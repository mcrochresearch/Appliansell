# Food/diet app complaints: paywalls, pricing, ads, data, privacy, coaching algorithms, tone/psychology, integrations

Scope covers MyFitnessPal (MFP), Noom, Lose It!, Cronometer, Yazio, FatSecret, MacroFactor and Carbon Diet Coach, plus what users praise in the apps they moved to.

**Source caveats for the writer, applying to every section:**
- **Reddit and Hacker News pages could not be read.** reddit.com is blocked to the search and fetch tools, and every WebFetch failed with DNS errors, including for news.ycombinator.com, community.myfitnesspal.com and unstar.app. Every finding below therefore comes from search-engine snippets and summaries of the cited pages. Nothing was verified against the full page text. Treat quoted phrases as close paraphrases unless they are marked as a quote.
- **Many 2025–2026 sources are blogs run by competing calorie apps.** These include nutrola.app, nutriscan.app, caleye.fit, bentobunny.app, gainframe.app, amyfoodjournal.com, unstar.app, kibora.app, caloriecue.app, fitia.app, growfyt.com and forkmate.ai. Each is tagged **[competitor blog]**. Use them as corroboration only, never as the sole basis for a statistic.
- **"Single anecdote"** marks a complaint that rests on one user's post or review.

## 1. Paywall and pricing anger: features moved to premium, subscription prices, cancellation dark patterns, free-tier limits, demand for a lifetime purchase

### Takeaway
MFP is the clearest case of features being taken away. The barcode scanner moved behind Premium in 2022, and Premium now costs $19.99 a month or $79.99 a year, about double its earlier price. Noom is the standard example of cancellation dark patterns: it paid a $62M class-action settlement in 2022, and complaints about charges after cancelling continued through 2025–2026. The pricing complaint about MacroFactor and Carbon is different. People object less to features being removed and more to the absence of any free tier and to $12–15 a month for a tool they must use every day.

### Cited Findings
**MyFitnessPal**
- **Barcode scanner moved to Premium (2022).** A Hacker News thread was titled "MyFitnessPal Barcode Scanner Goes Premium and What to Use Now" (item 32593833, 2022). It was **flagged as older**, and its comments could not be read. — [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=32593833)
- A user on a 2023 Slickdeals promo thread complained that MFP "put it behind a pay wall and is part of their premium membership (it wasn't before)". — [Slickdeals](https://slickdeals.net/f/16599074-instacart-users-free-3-months-of-myfitnesspal-premium-0)
- **Price increases.** A March 2023 reply from MFP staff on the community forum confirmed US pricing of "$19.99/monthly renewal rate or pay up front for a yearly renewal of $79.99". — [MFP Community "Price increase?"](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10847704/price-increase)
  - Another forum thread asks whether Premium is worth $80. — [MFP Community "Premium worth $80?"](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10830513/premium-worth-80)
  - A non-US user reported the monthly price going "from 9.99 a month now it's 19.99 a month". — [MFP Community "Is this for real??"](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10886194/is-this-for-real)
- **Current tiers.** Free, Premium at $79.99/yr or $19.99/mo, and Premium+ at $99.99/yr or $24.99/mo. Premium unlocks Barcode Scan, Meal Scan and Voice Log. — [Fitbudd](https://www.fitbudd.com/post/myfitnesspal-app-cost); [mynetdiary.com MFP alternatives](https://www.mynetdiary.com/myfitnesspal-alternatives.html) **[competitor blog]**
- **Private-equity sale.** Competitor blogs link the rising prices to MFP's 2020 sale to Francisco Partners, describing a move from $9.99 to $19.99 a month. Their price histories disagree: one lists a $14.99 step in 2020. — [Nutrola price history](https://nutrola.app/en/blog/why-did-myfitnesspal-increase-their-price) **[competitor blog]**
- **Share of reviews citing the paywall.** One analysis of recent app-store reviews says 29% of negative MFP reviews mention the paywall and 13% name the scanner. Sample size and method are unverified. — [Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/is-myfitnesspal-premium-worth-it-paywall-app-reviews-2026) **[competitor blog]**
- **CSV export is Premium-only.** This is covered under section 3.

**Noom**
- **Class-action settlement.** In *Mahood v. Noom* (S.D.N.Y. 1:20-cv-03677), Noom settled in February 2022 for $56M in cash plus $6M in subscription credits. **Flagged as older (2022).**
  - Plaintiffs alleged that "risk-free" trials turned into hard-to-cancel auto-renewals.
  - Users had to message a "coach" to cancel, and that coach was allegedly a bot.
  - A former engineer described cancellation as "difficult by design".
  - Noom denied wrongdoing. It agreed to clearer auto-renewal disclosures, a separate check-box or signature to consent to auto-renewal, and a "cancel" button on the account page.
  - Sources: [Globe and Mail](https://theglobeandmail.com/business/international-business/article-noom-diet-app-reaches-us62-million-settlement-over-automatic); [Kelley Drye Ad Law Access](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/noom-to-pay-over-60m-to-cancel-automatic-renewal-suit); [Deceptive.design case file](https://www.deceptive.design/cases/geraldine-mahood-v-noom-inc); [Wikipedia – Noom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noom)
- **No FTC action found.** The search did not find an FTC action specifically against Noom. The $62M was a private class action.
- **Charges after cancelling, 2025–2026 (BBB).** One complainant cancelled by phone and was still charged in October 2025 and again in June 2026. Noom's BBB replies show some refunds. — [BBB Noom complaints](https://www.bbb.org/us/ny/new-york/profile/health-and-wellness/noom-inc-0121-150555/complaints)
- **ConsumerAffairs.** One reviewer says they were charged $280 despite cancelling well before the trial ended. Noom replied in November 2025 that it had issued a full refund. — [ConsumerAffairs Noom p.2](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/health/noom.html?page=2)
- **PissedConsumer.** Noom has about 2,400 reviews averaging 1.5 stars (last updated November 2025). Many titles describe post-cancellation charges, including double charges. The site is self-selected. — [PissedConsumer](https://noom.pissedconsumer.com/review.html)
- **Unstar count.** In 906 negative reviews, Unstar found 24 describing charges after cancellation, a median cost of $184, renewals with no reminder, and refused refunds. — [Unstar Noom cost](https://unstar.app/blog/noom-cost-charged-after-cancelling-refund-reviews-2026) **[competitor blog; method unverified]**
- **Trial converted to a 3-month charge.** One reviewer signed up for a free trial, was charged for a 3-month subscription and was refused a refund. **Single anecdote.** — [ConsumerAffairs Noom](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/health/noom.html)

**MacroFactor**
- **No free tier.** There is a 7-day trial (some sources say 14 days), then $11.99/mo, $47.99 per 6 months or $71.99/yr. Reviewers call "subscription-only, no free tier" the complaint they see most often.
  - App Store reviewers object that you cannot try the app without subscribing, and one calls charging trial users who forget to cancel "predatory".
  - Sources: [Bento Bunny](https://www.bentobunny.app/reviews/macrofactor-review); [gainframe](https://gainframe.app/blog/macrofactor-review/); [justuseapp MacroFactor reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1553503471/macrofactor-diet-sidekick/reviews) **[mixed competitor / aggregator]**
- **Company rationale.** MacroFactor's stated reason is that an ad-free, paid model keeps its incentives aligned with users' results rather than with engagement. Reviewers relay this; it is not the company's own text. — [gainframe](https://gainframe.app/blog/macrofactor-review/)
- **App Store reviewer pushback.** One reviewer: "there is no feature in MacroFactor that is worth paying for", and free apps give similar results. **Single anecdote.** — [justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1553503471/macrofactor-diet-sidekick/reviews)

**Carbon Diet Coach**
- **Price.** About $14.99/mo according to one blog. — [Nutrola Carbon](https://nutrola.app/en/blog/is-carbon-diet-coach-worth-it-2026) **[competitor blog]**

**Yazio**
- **Most-cited complaint: the paywall.** Historical statistics and analysis tools sit behind PRO, and upsell reminders are frequent.
  - One comparison says Yazio's free tier is the most restricted of the group and puts macro tracking behind the paywall, which it calls a "dealbreaker".
  - Other complaints: auto-renewal and gaps in the US food database.
  - Pro costs about €7.99/mo.
  - Sources: [growfyt Yazio alternatives](https://www.growfyt.com/en/blog/alternativas-a-yazio); [Nutrola FatSecret vs Lose It vs Yazio](https://www.nutrola.app/en/blog/fatsecret-vs-lose-it-vs-yazio-free-tier-2026) **[competitor blogs]**

**Lose It!**
- **Pricing and free tier.** Complaints mention a "premium push", but the free tier is often described as "genuinely functional — not a trial in disguise". Premium costs $39.99/yr or $9.99/mo, roughly half of MFP. — [amyfoodjournal Lose It review](https://www.amyfoodjournal.com/blog/lose-it-app-review); [Unstar 1-star ranking](https://unstar.app/blog/calorie-tracking-apps-ranked-1-star-reviews-2026) **[competitor blogs]**

**FatSecret**
- **Free tier.** Widely praised as generous. Premium costs about $6.99/mo. — [Nutrola comparison](https://www.nutrola.app/en/blog/fatsecret-vs-lose-it-vs-yazio-free-tier-2026) **[competitor blog]**

**Cronometer**
- **No middle option.** Cronometer forum users say the choice is "free with invasive ads or ~$50/yr" and ask for a cheaper middle tier. Undated, possibly older. — [Cronometer forum "Remove Full Page Ads"](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/comment/19563)
- **Gold price.** Sources disagree: $59.99/yr or $10.99/mo per [Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/cronometer-review/), versus $5.99–8.99/mo in competitor blogs.

**Lifetime purchase demand**
- One indie MFP alternative (FitBee) added a one-time purchase after launching on subscription only. This was a developer's own post on Hacker News. — [HN Show HN: FitBee](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42313349)

### Inferences
- Users are angriest about losses: a feature that used to be free (MFP's scanner) or money taken after cancelling (Noom). A price that is merely high draws less anger. Apps that were always paid (MacroFactor) mostly get "let me try before I pay" complaints rather than accusations of betrayal.
- Lose It at about $40/yr and FatSecret's generous free tier act as reference points against which MFP's $80/yr looks expensive.
- Legally and reputationally, the safe pattern is easy self-serve cancellation, explicit consent to auto-renewal, and a reminder before a trial converts. The Noom settlement required the first two.

### Gaps
- No Reddit threads could be read directly, including r/MyFitnessPal outrage threads about the 2022 scanner paywall and r/noom cancellation threads.
- Trustpilot ratings and counts for Noom and MFP were not retrieved.
- No reliable source quantifies how often users ask for a lifetime purchase.
- Final court approval of the Noom settlement was not confirmed in the results seen.

## 2. Ads and performance: ad load, slowness, crashes, sync failures, data loss, outages

### Takeaway
Full-screen, unskippable interstitial ads in MFP's free tier, which appear right when the user is logging, are the central ad complaint for 2024–2026. Users link them to the app feeling slow. Cronometer's free tier has a similar but smaller full-page-ad complaint. Few large outages are documented. Third-party sync backlogs (Fitbit and Garmin to MFP) recur.

### Cited Findings
**Ads**
- **MFP unskippable ads.** A 2025 critic says ads have "become increasingly aggressive to the point where a quick log-in to save food means watching a full-screen, unskippable ad". — [FeastGood "MyFitnessPal Sucks"](https://feastgood.com/myfitnesspal-sucks/)
- **MFP interstitials.** Full-screen ads appear between logging food and switching tabs, and video ads appear periodically. One blog claims "6–12 ads per session"; that figure is unverified and should be discounted. — [Nutrola MFP ads](https://nutrola.app/en/blog/myfitnesspal-ads-too-many-free-alternatives-without-ads-2026) **[competitor blog]**
- **MFP slowness.** A Trustpilot reviewer (3 stars) wrote "sometimes system very slow probably due to adverts". Other Trustpilot reviews mention sluggishness, crashes, and trouble finding or updating saved meals and recipes. A further reviewer wrote that the app has "loads of ads but I simply ignore them". — [Trustpilot MFP](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/www.myfitnesspal.com)
- **Fitbudd on MFP ads.** The blog says the ads "slow down the app and make the user experience clunky". This is opinion. — [Fitbudd](https://www.fitbudd.com/post/myfitnesspal-app-cost)
- **Cronometer full-page ads (forum, undated, possibly older).** One free user said banner ads were fine but full-page ads that block the app for several seconds would make them uninstall rather than pay. Another user had a full-screen ad pop up right as they entered the logging screen. — [Cronometer forum](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/comment/19563)
- **Cronometer bug report.** Ads blocked screen elements and the app had to be restarted. Staff later said a release fixed it. — [Cronometer forum bug](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/comment/8801/)
- **Cronometer "ad crisis" (2025–2026).** One 1-star-review roundup describes a surge of complaints about full-screen video ads in the middle of logging a meal, which "hijack the app for up to half a minute". — [Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/calorie-tracking-apps-ranked-1-star-reviews-2026) **[competitor blog]**
  - Contradiction: other blogs call Cronometer's free-tier ads "minimal" or say there are none. — [hootfitness](https://www.hootfitness.com/blog/cronometer-alternatives-find-the-best-fit-for-your-tracking-style)
- **FatSecret and Yazio ads.** FatSecret ads appear "throughout the free version" but are "visible but not blocking". Yazio's ads are "moderate". Lose It's ads are "non-intrusive banners". — [Nutrola comparison](https://www.nutrola.app/en/blog/fatsecret-vs-lose-it-vs-yazio-free-tier-2026); [kibora](https://kibora.app/blog/best-free-calorie-counter-app/) **[competitor blogs]**
- **Older HN complaints about MFP speed.** Commenters called MFP "way too slow" and "horribly written". **Flagged as older (2018–2019).** — [HN 21888185](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21888185); [HN 16711318](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=16711318)

**Outages and sync**
- **MFP incidents found:**
  - March 2024: barcode data corruption, where a subset of barcode foods had the wrong brand and name and needed remediation.
  - April 2024: about a 2-hour site outage during a system migration, which degraded app features.
  - 26 November 2024: about a 1-hour outage of the logged-in web home page.
  - Sources: [StatusGator MFP](https://statusgator.com/services/myfitnesspal); [IsDown MFP](https://isdown.app/status/myfitnesspal)
- **Fitbit to MFP sync backlog (undated).** A Fitbit reply explained that "every sync request ... gets added to the back of the line", which left users waiting weeks. Another thread reports 2–3 weeks without a sync. — [Fitbit community](https://community.fitbit.com/t5/Third-Party-Integrations/MyFitnessPal-Syncing-Issues/m-p/4351298/highlight/true)
- **Garmin to MFP sync (older threads).** Garmin forum threads "myfitnesspal sync still broken" and "unable to sync myfitnesspal". — [Garmin forum](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-web/85118/myfitnesspal-sync-still-broken); [Garmin forum 2](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-web/265691/unable-to-sync-myfitnesspal)
- **Android Authority troubleshooting guide.** It exists for MFP problems, which suggests the problems are widespread. Contents were not read. — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/myfitnesspal-problems-fixes-3251396)

### Inferences
- Ads timed at the moment of logging are the worst kind for a logging app. They add friction to the core habit, and users read the friction as "slow app".
- "No ads, ever" is a selling point that alternatives such as MacroFactor and indie apps use explicitly (see section 7).

### Gaps
- No verified, dated 2025–2026 source on MFP or Cronometer crash or data-loss incidents (diary entries disappearing) beyond the snippets above.
- No measured data on ad load or app performance. Only opinion was found.
- No information found on offline logging behaviour for any of these apps.

## 3. Data ownership and privacy: export, import, account deletion, data sharing, breaches, offline use

### Takeaway
MFP locks its convenient CSV export behind Premium, though a GDPR-style "download my data" request is reportedly free on the web. Its export is per meal, not per food. The 2018 MFP breach of about 150M accounts is the defining privacy event. 2025 analyses single out Noom as collecting the most data types among weight-loss apps. Developer threads on Hacker News show demand for apps that need no account and avoid data-sharing.

### Cited Findings
- **2018 MFP breach (flagged as older).** Under Armour disclosed in March 2018 that about 150M MFP accounts were breached in late February 2018. Exposed data was usernames, email addresses and hashed passwords, mostly bcrypt. No payment-card data or government IDs were taken. — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/security-and-risk/2018/under-armour-data-breach-myfitnesspal-hack-cyberattack/); [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/70814/data-breach/under-armour-hack.html); [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/under-armour-says-150-million-myfitnesspal-user-accounts-were-breached-1.1042133)
- **MFP CSV export is Premium-only.** The MFP help center: "Premium users can use the file export to download meal nutrition details, progress history, and exercise history". — [MFP Support Data Export FAQs](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032273352-Data-Export-FAQs)
- **Export granularity.** The export groups data by meal and time; "MyFitnessPal does not allow you to export on a food level". — [Quantified Self](https://quantifiedself.com/blog/access-export-myfitnesspal-data/)
- **Free workarounds.** Free users can print or share their diary. A GDPR "Download My Data" request is reportedly free on the website only. Sources disagree on its format (password-protected .xlsx versus a ZIP of CSVs). — [Forkmate](https://forkmate.ai/blog/export-myfitnesspal-data-free/) **[competitor blog]**
- **Third-party converters.** Converters such as the PyPI package myFitnessPal-Converter exist, which signals that users need to migrate their data. — [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/myFitnessPal-Converter/)
- **Export as a general complaint.** A Hacker News commenter: "the fact that you can't export your data is my biggest gripe with fitness trackers". — [HN 40833944](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40833944)
- **Noom data collection (Surfshark, January 2025).** Among weight-control apps, Noom "stands out" by collecting seven data types for tracking. Lose It!, Fastic, Noom, WeightWatchers and Simple fall in the group with above-average tracking. — [Surfshark research](https://surfshark.com/research/chart/weight-control-apps-privacy); [Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2025/01/your-weight-loss-app-might-be-spying-on.html)
- **MFP data collection.** A summary of similar research says MFP collects 10 unique data types but uses only 2 for tracking. — [Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2025/01/your-weight-loss-app-might-be-spying-on.html)
- **HIPAA (single anecdote).** A Privacy Guides forum user notes that Noom's health data is not protected by HIPAA because it is a consumer app. — [Privacy Guides forum](https://discuss.privacyguides.net/t/please-suggest-a-privacy-respecting-health-and-nutrition-and-activity-app-on-android/30657)
- **No-account apps.** An "Ask HN: Any calorie-tracking app without the need for an account?" thread shows demand. — [HN 40120488](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40120488)
  - Alternatives developers pitch on HN:
    - FoodNoms: no account; diary stored in the user's own iCloud.
    - Waistline: open source, uses OpenFoodFacts.
    - MyNetDiary: optional account, "no user data sharing" (2020 post).
    - FitBee: Sign in with Apple, no ads.
  - These are self-promotion by the developers. — [HN 40833944](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40833944); [HN 42313349](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42313349)
- **Mozilla.** Mozilla's *Privacy Not Included* has no review of MFP, Noom or Lose It in the results. Its coverage found was mental-health and prayer apps. — [Mozilla Foundation](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/blog/getting-private-mental-health-and-prayer-apps-rush-to-revise-privacy-policies/)
- **Bitdefender** has an article on why calorie-tracking data attracts hackers; its contents were not read. — [Bitdefender](https://bitdefender.in/?p=89562)

### Inferences
- Charging for an export ("pay to leave") is felt as lock-in and is a clear opening for competitors. A free, food-level CSV export plus importers from MFP, Cronometer and Lose It would answer it directly.
- Local-first or no-account options and a clear "we don't share or sell" stance appeal to the privacy-minded group. On HN, privacy is often the main reason people adopt an alternative.

### Gaps
- No source confirms what MFP or Noom actually delete when an account is deleted, or how long they keep data.
- No reliable Mozilla, Consumer Reports or academic privacy rating for these specific apps was found.
- Import tools in MacroFactor, Cronometer and others were not confirmed. Competitor blogs claim MFP import, but this was not verified.
- Offline-use complaints were not found.

## 4. Coaching and targets: low or generic targets, adaptive TDEE, eating back exercise calories, weekly budgets, macros, diet breaks

### Takeaway
Users criticise static, formula-based targets, especially MFP's default floor of about 1,200 kcal for women, as generic or too low. The "net calories" design that encourages eating back exercise calories causes confusion and double counting. Adaptive-expenditure apps (MacroFactor, Carbon) get praise for adjusting to real weight trends. MacroFactor in particular is praised for not depending on adherence. Their complaints are about speed: adjustments come too slowly (weekly only) or too aggressively when the scale stalls. Weekly budgets and calorie roll-over are requested features that MacroFactor already supports.

### Cited Findings
- **MFP's 1,200/1,500 floor.** MFP says it does "not recommend women consume fewer than 1200 calories per day, or men fewer than 1500". — [MFP Support initial goals](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032625391-How-does-MyFitnessPal-calculate-my-initial-goals)
  - Critics argue that MFP often defaults women to 1,200, "WAY lower than most women will ever need". — [aworkoutroutine](https://www.aworkoutroutine.com/eating-back-calories-burned/)
  - BuzzFeed News on the cultural origin of 1,200: [BuzzFeed](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/scaachikoul/1200-calorie-diet-body-week) **(older)**
- **Under-1,200 warning confusion.** Users argue over whether MFP's "not eating enough" warning counts net or gross calories, and one suspects a bug. **Flagged as old (about 12 years).** — [MFP Community](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10014602/1200-not-eating-enough-message)
- **Cronometer recommendations below 1,000 kcal.** A forum user worried the app recommended under 1,000 kcal and could mislead people into dangerous under-eating. — [Cronometer forum](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/comment/16369)
- **Net-calorie model and eating back.** MFP's model is "eat 2,500, burn 500 = net 2,000", which nudges users to eat back exercise calories. — [MFP Support](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032625391-How-does-MyFitnessPal-calculate-my-initial-goals)
  - Lose It users ask how to stop workout calories being added back to the daily budget. — [Glassdoor community Q](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Community/staying-healthy/anyone-using-the-lose-it-app-and-suggest-how-to-stop-the-exercise-calories-to-be-accounted-back-into-the-calories-for-the-day) **(single anecdote)**
- **MacroFactor's adherence-neutral design.** Targets update based on actual intake and weight changes, not on how closely the user stuck to the plan. The design is meant to make slips "less psychologically stressful". — [MacroFactor](https://macrofactor.com/macrofactor/)
  - An App Store reviewer: other apps need you to adhere before they update, "which makes it easy to quit and then binge", whereas MacroFactor gives valid targets as long as you keep logging. — [App Store MacroFactor](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/macrofactor-macro-tracker/id1553503471)
  - A long-time user (two or more years) praises the expenditure trend. — [FeastGood MacroFactor review](https://feastgood.com/macrofactor-review/)
- **MacroFactor criticisms.**
  - You pay "for an engine you're not running" unless you weigh in and log consistently. — [gainframe](https://gainframe.app/blog/macrofactor-review/)
  - It is "a numbers instrument rather than a coach", not suited to people who only want occasional awareness of calories. — [amyfoodjournal MacroFactor](https://www.amyfoodjournal.com/blog/macrofactor-review) **[competitor blog]**
  - An accuracy claim of "±9% in a 2026 validation study" by the "Dietary Assessment Initiative" appears only in a competitor blog. The study could not be verified; **treat as suspect.** — [bestcalorieapps](https://bestcalorieapps.com/en/reviews/macrofactor/)
- **Weekly budget and roll-over.**
  - MacroFactor's help center describes a fixed weekly budget: a higher day lowers the other days.
  - It acknowledges that "many users would like to 'roll over' unused calories" and offers this through Collaborative programs.
  - Sources: [MacroFactor Weekly Budget](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/92-weekly-budget); [MacroFactor roll-over article](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/108-how-to-adjust-calorie-targets-to-account-for-overages-or-to-roll-over-unused-calories)
  - A feature-request thread asking for a weekly calorie target with "remaining calories for the week" was endorsed by several users. — [ABC Trainerize ideas](https://ideas.abcfitness.com/forums/167887-coach-trainer-abc-trainerize/suggestions/43386366-setting-weekly-caloric-goal-for-calorie-banking)
  - Lose It works on a daily budget (2018 JMIR study). — [JMIR mHealth 2018](https://mhealth.jmir.org/2018/4/e83/PDF)
- **Carbon Diet Coach complaints.**
  - Adjustments happen only at the weekly check-in, with no mid-week changes. — [caleye Carbon vs MacroFactor](https://caleye.fit/blog/carbon-vs-macrofactor-2026/) **[competitor blog]**
  - "Weeks ... where they clearly missed their macros and nothing has changed"; the coach seems to wait for manual input. — [garagegymrevisited](https://garagegymrevisited.com/carbon-diet-coach/)
  - It "keeps trying to lower calories too low if they don't lose weight on the scale every week". — [Carbon App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/1437820611?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone) **(single anecdote)**
  - Other cons cited: retroactive adjustments, poor food search on Google Play, and no recipes, meal plans or workouts. — [FeastGood Carbon review](https://feastgood.com/carbon-diet-coach-review/); [Google Play Carbon](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.joincarbon.nutrition&hl=en_US)
- **Carbon praise.** Users can see the history of check-ins and why each change was made. One reviewer liked that Carbon did not change targets when they had not hit them, while another found the same behaviour frustrating. — [App Store Carbon](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/carbon-macro-coach-tracker/id1437820611?l=pt-BR); [FeastGood](https://feastgood.com/carbon-diet-coach-review/)
- **Noom coaching.** Coaching is called "impersonal and scripted", and coaches were "young and didn't seem to fully grasp my situation (over 60/postmenopausal)". Many users say replies "seemed like talking to a bot". — [ConsumerAffairs Noom](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/health/noom.html); [Scaled and Failed Substack](https://scaledandfailed.substack.com/p/issue-23-psych-fitness)

### Inferences
- Users want three things: targets based on their own trend data, an explanation for every change (Carbon's check-in history is praised for this), and no punishment for imperfect adherence.
- A tension exists between adjusting too slowly (Carbon waits for adherence) and too aggressively (cutting calories after one stalled week). Smoothing with trend weight, plus a floor on targets, addresses both.
- Exercise calories should be explicit and configurable (eat none, a share, or all of them back) so they are not silently added to the budget.

### Gaps
- No user-sourced data on diet breaks or maintenance phases as features, beyond MacroFactor's own program types.
- Complaints about macro targets (for example protein set too low or too high) were not specifically found.

## 5. Psychology and tone: shame, red numbers, guilt, streak anxiety, eating-disorder triggers, gamification, nagging notifications

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed studies link MFP use to self-reported contribution to eating disorders: about 73% of ED patients who used MFP said it at least "somewhat" contributed. Users ask for a mode that hides calorie numbers in Cronometer, Garmin and open-source trackers. MacroFactor markets the absence of red numbers, warnings and shaming as a differentiator. Streaks and reminders are optional in Lose It, but commentators criticise the "budget" and "you went over" framing.

### Cited Findings
- **MFP and eating disorders (Levinson et al. 2017).** Among ED patients who used MFP, 73.1% said it at least somewhat contributed to their ED, and 62.9% said at least moderately. The sample was small, the data self-reported, and causation was not shown. **Flagged as older.** — [ScienceDirect, Eating Behaviors 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1471015317301484)
- **Messer et al. 2021 (n=1,357).** Former or current users of calorie apps reported more thinness- and muscularity-oriented disordered eating. People tracking for weight or shape reasons more often said the app fed food preoccupation, all-or-none thinking, food anxiety and purging. — [ScienceDirect, Eating Behaviors 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1471015321000957)
- **2024 systematic review.** About half of participants reported negative experiences with apps, such as obsessive counting, restriction, guilt and low mood. — [ScienceDirect, Body Image 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S174014452400158X)
- **Counter-evidence.** An RCT in undergraduate women found that introducing a calorie-counting app had "no effect on mental health or health behaviors". — [ScienceDirect, JAND 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212267221007346)
- **Hide-calories requests.**
  - Cronometer forum: people with eating disorders "are frequently advised by their doctors or psychiatrists to abstain from counting calories". — [Cronometer forum](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/comment/11651)
  - OpenNutriTracker GitHub issue #1312 asks for a display mode that hides kcal, carbs and fat and shows user-chosen nutrients such as fibre, protein or iron. — [GitHub](https://github.com/simonoppowa/OpenNutriTracker/issues/1312)
  - Garmin forum: an option to hide weight and calorie burn, keeping them "out of sight, out of mind". Another commenter pushed back. — [Garmin forum](https://forums.garmin.com/outdoor-recreation/outdoor-recreation/f/fenix-6-series/273772/request-ability-to-hide-weight-calorie-burn-data-in-watch-and-in-app)
- **Recovered users' warnings.** People recovered from eating disorders describe calorie apps as dangerous (2023 press). Beat, the UK ED charity, has pushed for screening and signposting to support. — [Tyla 2023](https://www.tyla.com/health/calorie-counting-apps-impact-eating-disorders-354995-20230403)
- **MacroFactor's tone.** Its App Store listing says users never see "warnings, red numbers, or shaming" when they exceed a target. A 2026 review says it avoids "red numbers, streak-shaming, and budget-scolding". This is mostly the company's own marketing. — [App Store MacroFactor](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/macrofactor-macro-tracker/id1553503471); [gainframe](https://gainframe.app/blog/macrofactor-review/)
- **Lose It streaks and framing.**
  - Lose It promotes streaks as "a daily reminder you're working toward something bigger" (on X). — [Lose It on X](https://x.com/loseit/status/2037208713748971803)
  - A competitor blog says Lose It sends several daily reminders by default, which can be turned off under Settings > Reminders.
  - The same blog says the "budget" language frames eating as accounting.
  - It also argues that messages like "You went over!" or "You broke your streak" can amplify food anxiety.
  - Source: [Nutrola notifications](https://nutrola.app/en/blog/calorie-tracker-notifications-too-annoying-how-to-customize) **[competitor blog]**
- **Streak guilt outside food apps.** An analogous essay on Duolingo argues that the guilt of losing a streak makes people quit, and suggests showing total days rather than consecutive days. — [Medium](https://saunved.medium.com/why-i-let-go-of-my-480-day-duolingo-streak-a2098b3eff35)
- **Noom psychology lessons.** No source found calls them "condescending". The closest complaints are that the lessons taught nothing new, and "the lessons are great ... the coaching and food logging leave a lot to be desired". — [Fortune Noom review](https://fortune.com/article/noom-review); [millennialhawk](https://millennialhawk.com/noom-review/)

### Inferences
- A neutral palette with no red, a "hide numbers" or nutrient-focus mode, "total days logged" in place of streaks, quiet notifications by default, and adherence-neutral coaching together form a known set of differentiators. MacroFactor already sells several of them.
- Screening at the point of setting a goal, for example when the user enters a very low target or BMI, is an emerging expectation (Beat's advocacy).

### Gaps
- No experimental evidence was found that hiding numbers or removing red changes harm. Requests are anecdotal.
- No r/EDAnonymous or r/fuckeatingdisorders threads could be read because Reddit is blocked.
- No primary source documents Noom's tone being called patronising.

## 6. Integrations: Apple Health, Google Fit, Garmin and Fitbit sync complaints, double-counted steps and exercise calories

### Takeaway
Double counting is the persistent integration complaint. The same workout or steps arrive from several sources (Garmin directly and via Apple Health, Strava, or Google Fit), or are synced and also logged by hand. MFP's "calorie adjustment" logic confuses users, and third-party sync queues lag by days or weeks.

### Cited Findings
- **MFP community double-counting threads.** "Double counting calories", "Are my steps being double counted?", "Garmin Connect calorie adjustment confusing!" and "Garmin Connect Frustrations". — [MFP Community 1](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10576575/double-counting-calories); [2](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10890296/are-my-steps-being-double-counted); [3](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10835692/garmin-connect-calorie-adjustment-confusing); [4](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10906695/garmin-connect-frustrations)
- **How MFP's Garmin adjustment works.** MFP takes Garmin's daily burn and subtracts the burn it expected from the user's activity level. It also subtracts synced workouts. A misconfiguration double-counts workouts. Users report that enabling "negative adjustments" fixed it. — [MFP Community](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10835692/garmin-connect-calorie-adjustment-confusing)
- **Google Fit plus Garmin.** One user had both feeding MFP and had to disable one. — [MFP Community](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10576575/double-counting-calories)
- **Garmin to Apple Health doubling (flagged as older, about 6 years).** Apple Health showed double the calories and steps after a Garmin Connect update. — [Garmin forum](https://forums.garmin.com/apps-software/mobile-apps-web/f/garmin-connect-mobile-ios/214861/apple-health-displays-double-the-calories-and-steps-after-physical-activity)
- **Cronometer duplicate workouts.** Users report duplicate workouts and suspect "double-dipping" from Apple Health. The advice was to unlink one source. — [Cronometer forum duplicates](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/comment/9391); [Cronometer double-dipping](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/comment/21399)
- **Why Apple Health doubles.** Apple Health stores every entry from every device, so a Garmin workout that arrives directly and also via Strava gets doubled. — [AI Food Coach blog](https://aifoodcoach.app/blog/apple-health-duplicate-calories-fix) **[competitor blog]**
- **Sync lag.** Fitbit to MFP sync can be weeks behind. — [Fitbit community](https://community.fitbit.com/t5/Third-Party-Integrations/MyFitnessPal-Syncing-Issues/m-p/4351298/highlight/true)
- **Open API demand (flagged as older).** HN users ask for MFP alternatives with an open API, because MFP restricted its API. — [HN 16711842](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=16711842)

### Inferences
- An integration layer that de-duplicates by source priority and shows which source each burn figure came from would address a long-running pain point. So would a single setting for how many exercise calories to add back.

### Gaps
- Most integration threads are undated or older than 2023. No 2025–2026 incident reports were confirmed.
- No sources were found on Health Connect (Android) issues specifically.

## 7. What users praise in the alternatives they moved to

### Takeaway
People leaving MFP cite five reasons, each pointing to an alternative:
- **Cost:** Lose It at about $40/yr; FatSecret's free tier.
- **No ads, or no paywall on basics:** MacroFactor; indie apps.
- **Data quality and micronutrients:** Cronometer.
- **Adaptive, judgment-free coaching:** MacroFactor, Carbon.
- **Privacy or no account:** FoodNoms, Waistline, FitBee.

### Cited Findings
- **MacroFactor.**
  - Users praise its adherence-neutral targets, the absence of red numbers or shaming, and the fact that it has no ads.
  - Former MFP users say they realised MFP was "outdated" only after switching.
  - It has about a 4.8 App Store rating from roughly 19,500 ratings, per a review site.
  - Sources: [justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1553503471/macrofactor-diet-sidekick/reviews); [Bento Bunny](https://www.bentobunny.app/reviews/macrofactor-review); [Lemon8 user post](https://www.lemon8-app.com/@skincait/7231939022086472193?region=sg) **(anecdote)**
- **Cronometer.** It is praised for data accuracy and micronutrient tracking, but criticised as feeling "like a research tool, not a consumer app". — [hootfitness](https://www.hootfitness.com/blog/cronometer-alternatives-find-the-best-fit-for-your-tracking-style)
- **Lose It!** Users praise its functional free tier and lower price. — [amyfoodjournal](https://www.amyfoodjournal.com/blog/lose-it-app-review)
- **FatSecret.** Users praise its generous free features, including the barcode scanner. The UI is "not meaningfully modernized since the mid-2010s", and its crowd-sourced database has accuracy gaps. HN users recommended it as early as 2016 (flagged as older). — [HN 13130681](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=13130681); [Nutrola comparison](https://www.nutrola.app/en/blog/fatsecret-vs-lose-it-vs-yazio-free-tier-2026)
- **Carbon.** Users praise the transparency of its check-ins and its responsive customer service. — [FeastGood Carbon](https://feastgood.com/carbon-diet-coach-review/)
- **Indie and privacy apps on HN.**
  - FoodNoms: no account, data in iCloud.
  - Waistline: open source.
  - FitBee: no ads, USDA data, Sign in with Apple.
  - HN threads include "A fast, simple and privacy friendly MyFitnessPal alternative" (2024) and a "Show HN" MFP alternative (2025).
  - Sources: [HN 40833944](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40833944); [HN 42595864](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42595864); [HN 42313349](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42313349)
- **AI photo-logging apps (counterpoint).** An HN thread titled "I used AI-powered calorie counting apps, and they were even worse than expected" (2025). Users also complained that they could not export meal photos. — [HN 44220135](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44220135)

### Inferences
- No single competitor combines all of the following: a free barcode scanner, no intrusive ads, a free full export and import, adaptive and judgment-free targets, and privacy. That combination is the gap.

### Gaps
- No quantitative churn or switching data, such as the share of users leaving MFP for each alternative, was found.
- Reddit "I switched to X" threads could not be accessed, so the praise comes mostly from review sites and App Store snippets.
