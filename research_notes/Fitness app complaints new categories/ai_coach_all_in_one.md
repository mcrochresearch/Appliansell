# Complaints about AI-coach, human-coach and all-in-one fitness apps (round 2: memory, injuries, check-ins, tone, accountability, sprawl, onboarding)

**Source access note (read first).** The research environment's egress policy blocked direct fetches of reddit.com, apps.apple.com, techradar.com, gearpatrol.com, androidpolice.com, easternherald.com and unstar.app (proxy returned 403 / DNS failure, 2026-10-09). **Every quote below is therefore secondhand**: it comes from search-engine snippets of the cited page, not from a page I read in full. Reddit threads are known only through journalism that quoted them (Gear Patrol, TechRadar, Fortune). Treat the quote wording as close but not guaranteed verbatim. "Widespread" ratings are my judgement: **High** = several outlets or a quantified review sample; **Medium** = one outlet reporting multiple users, or recurring across 2+ sources; **Low** = a single anecdote.

Round 1 already covered: AI coaches that should be grounded in the user's logs, black-box progression, and Noom's subscription settlement. This file avoids repeating those and adds new specifics.

Per-finding format: **App** — complaint ("user words") — widespread — *feature that would fix it (nütn)* — [Source](URL), date.

## 1. What do users complain about in AI coaches (fabricated numbers, injuries/equipment, memory, generic advice, refusals, tone, explanations, privacy, cost)?

### Takeaway
The most-reported AI-coach failure in 2025–2026 is the coach **inventing facts about the user's own data** (Whoop: fabricated elevation and night wakings; Fitbit Gemini: "already hallucinating"), followed by **context-blind, efficiency-maximising advice** that ignores the user's life (Fitbit coach suggesting the user "ditch the dog") and **no durable memory of injuries and history** (an industry gap Whoop only addressed with "My Memory" in May 2026). Privacy of health data sent to LLMs is a live journalism/advocacy concern, not yet a big user-review theme. I found **no evidence** of users complaining about over-cautious refusals. Tone complaints run in two directions: too pushy (Fitbit) and too empty or cheerleading (Strava, Apple Workout Buddy).

### Cited Findings

**Hallucinated or fabricated data about the user**
- **Whoop Coach (GPT-4)**: Reddit thread "Whoop AI is legit slop". The coach cited the user's elevation gain on a hike, and when asked where the number came from it admitted it had made the stat up. — Widespread: **Medium-High** (several r/whoop threads, covered by national press). — *Fix: every number in a coach reply is pulled from a log record and links back to it; the LLM is never allowed to state a metric that isn't in the retrieved context, and says "I don't have that data" otherwise.* — [Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/fitness/whoop-ai-reddit-user-feedback-pros-cons/) (secondhand, ~mid-2026; summarised via search snippet)
- **Whoop Coach**: Reddit thread "Whoop hallucinating". The coach admitted it had made up that the user was more awake at night, then used that invented detail to infer how the user felt. — **Medium-High**. — *Fix: cite the sleep record (date, wake count) beside any sleep claim; never infer mood from data you don't have, and ask instead.* — [Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/fitness/whoop-ai-reddit-user-feedback-pros-cons/)
- **Whoop Coach**: A commenter quit the coach after it "falsely assumed they were sick, then apologized when called out". — **Low-Medium**. — *Fix: hedge illness and health inferences ("your HRV is 18% below your 30-day baseline; that can mean illness, alcohol or poor sleep. Did anything change?") rather than stating a diagnosis.* — [Gear Patrol](https://www.gearpatrol.com/fitness/whoop-ai-reddit-user-feedback-pros-cons/)
- **Fitbit / Google Health Gemini coach** (Fitbit Air reviews, May 2026): 9to5Google reported the coach was "already hallucinating", with some recommendations "inaccurate or disconnected from real-world user behavior". — **Medium** (multiple reviewers per the roundup). — *Fix: same grounding rule; show the "based on" data window under each recommendation.* — [Eastern Herald roundup, relaying 9to5Google](https://easternherald.com/2026/05/26/google-fitbit-air-review-ai-health-coach-problems/) (secondhand twice; 2026-05-26)
- **Strava Athlete Intelligence**: In Oct 2024 a cyclist posted a ride on which he'd been hit by a car, and the AI praised his "consistent, well-rounded" fitness. Users joked it "feels more like a meme than anything right now". — **High** (viral; covered by Fortune and AOL). — *Fix: read the user's notes and journal text before summarising a session; flag sessions with injury or incident keywords for a check-in, not praise.* — [Fortune, 2024-10-11](https://fortune.com/2024/10/11/strava-app-artificial-intelligence-fitness-athletic-memes); [AOL](https://www.aol.com/finance/fitness-app-strava-ai-coach-110000016.html)
- **Strava Athlete Intelligence**: Android Central found it called a run averaging 150 bpm "anaerobic". A May 2026 forum thread still calls its summaries "unintentionally hilarious", including praise for a ride that was actually slow. — **Medium**. — *Fix: classify zones and effort deterministically (rules engine); the LLM only explains them.* — [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/wearables/strava-announces-stable-athlete-intelligence-anti-cheating-and-flyover-sharing); [Singletrack forum](https://singletrackworld.com/forum/bike-forum/ai-thinks-i-am-a-cycling-god-%f0%9f%a4%a3/)

**Context-blind or "efficiency-maximising" advice (life, dog, illness)**
- **Fitbit Gemini coach** (public preview, late 2025): A Reddit user who has walked their dog twice a day for years said that after explaining the slow pace was due to the dog sniffing, the coach asked if they could "ditch the dog to speed up" the walks. TechRadar says many others posted similar stories, and users said they "cannot wait for my trial to end". — **Medium-High** (TechRadar: "far from the only person"). — *Fix: let users tag activities with context such as "dog walk", "commute" or "with kids", and have the coach treat tagged activities as fixed, never as something to optimise.* — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/fitbits-gemini-ai-coach-is-giving-users-unhinged-fitness-advice-heres-why-users-are-saying-they-cannot-wait-for-my-trial-to-end) (secondhand)
- **Fitbit Gemini coach**: TechRadar's own reviewer said it fixated on a minor cold ("yesterday was a full recovery day with minimal movement") and that it "latches on to any context you give it", which can lead it astray. TechRadar says it is tuned "a little too strongly towards fitness efficiency and improvement". — **Medium**. — *Fix: context entries (illness, travel) have an end date and fade out; the user can say "I'm better now" and clear them.* — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/fitbits-gemini-ai-coach-is-giving-users-unhinged-fitness-advice-heres-why-users-are-saying-they-cannot-wait-for-my-trial-to-end)
- **Bevel (AI coach, "Bevel Intelligence")**: An engineer-reviewer found the insights "got vaguer during travel, launch weeks, or irregular sleep", that scores "don't show their inputs well", and that scores can look more precise than they are. — **Low-Medium** (one detailed review, echoed by others). — *Fix: a "why this score" breakdown listing each input and its contribution, and a "travel / irregular week" mode that widens baselines.* — [Autonomous.ai review](https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/bevel-app-review) (2025–26, secondhand)

**No memory of history or injuries; injury handling**
- **Fitbit Gemini coach**: Android Police warns the coach "may lack enough knowledge of your history" to know whether past injuries make a recommended plan risky. It also speculates that liability concerns may keep the plans generic and the advice "intentionally vague" (the author's inference, not Google policy). — **Medium** (journalist concern, not user reports). — *Fix: a persistent injury and limitations list (joint, side, movements that hurt, date) that both the rule-based coach and the LLM must read before suggesting exercises, with automatic swaps.* — [Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/things-to-consider-before-giving-fitbit-and-gemini-control-of-your-fitness/) (secondhand)
- **Fitbit Gemini coach (vendor claim)**: TechCrunch reported at launch that if you're injured mid-week "the AI will modify the plan accordingly". This was a described capability, not a tested one. — [TechCrunch, 2025-10-27](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/27/fitbits-revamped-app-with-gemini-powered-health-coach-rolls-out-to-premium-users/)
- **Whoop "My Memory"** (launched 2026-05-08), the market response to the memory gap: users add context by text or voice in categories Goals, Identity, Lifestyle, Preferences, Events, **Health History (injuries/illness)** and Mood. Each item can be edited, deleted or toggled off; temporary items such as a past illness are remembered "without actively coaching on them". It shipped alongside "Proactive Check-Ins". — *Implication: an editable, user-visible memory ledger is now table stakes for LLM coaches.* — [WHOOP Locker: My Memory](https://www.whoop.com/us/en/thelocker/my-memory-whoop/); [WHOOP press release 2026-05-08](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20260508464188/en/WHOOP-Expands-Health-Platform-with-On-Demand-Clinician-Access-and-New-AI-Features); [Gadgets & Wearables beta report](https://gadgetsandwearables.com/2026/05/08/whoop-memory/)
- **ChatGPT as a coach (memory)**: A 2026 vendor test says memory exists (Feb 2024) and chat-history reference followed (Apr 2025), but "a chat summary is a summary, not a set-by-set log", and nothing in ChatGPT sets next week's load from last week's sets. — **Medium** (a common pattern; the source is a competitor blog). — *Fix: nütn's advantage. The LLM coach reads the actual set log, so a progression suggestion cites the last session's sets and RPE.* — [Sensai blog](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/chatgpt-workout-plan) (vendor, 2026)
- **ChatGPT (memory loss)**: Tom's Guide reported ChatGPT users suddenly losing saved memories, so the assistant forgot "important personal information". — **Medium**. — *Fix: the coach's memory is the user's own on-device logs and profile, which are exportable and don't vanish with a model or account change.* — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/chatgpt/chatgpt-memories-are-disappearing-for-some-users-heres-how-to-protect-yours) (2025)
- **ChatGPT (injury and form)**: A clinical director and trainer quoted by Tom's Guide said AI can't assess "movement quality, injury history, mobility limitations" and that blind use is "a fast track to injury". NBC's trainer said "ChatGPT lacks specificity" and "is unable to assess form", though it did adapt a schedule when told the user was travelling. — **Medium** (expert opinion, not user reports). — *Fix: an injury list plus a pain flag on any set (one tap, "this hurt") that removes or substitutes that movement in future sessions; link to form cues and video.* — [Tom's Guide](https://tomsguide.com/wellness/fitness/following-a-chatgpt-training-program-can-be-ineffective-and-a-fast-track-to-injury-unless-you-follow-these-key-tips); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/chatgpt-used-personal-trainer-creating-workouts-rcna87970) (NBC date not shown in snippet, likely 2023)
- **General-purpose LLM coach (case study)**: In a single-subject half-marathon study (Jul–Sep 2025) the author lists limitations of "limited persistence, guardrails, and personalization". Support was "mostly user-initiated" and "weakest when the runner most needed it". The author proposes "persistent athlete models with safety-aware progression logic". — **Research, n=1**. — *Fix: a proactive, rule-triggered nudge when adherence drops (missed planned sessions, falling readiness), not only a chat box that waits for the user.* — [arXiv 2509.26593](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.26593v1)

**Generic or empty advice, tone and personality**
- **Apple Workout Buddy (watchOS 26)**: How-To Geek ("I Had High Hopes… but I Hate It Already") argues Apple's data could support far more than a pep talk, and that AI "should be able to do more than note that you've done two runs this week and congratulate you". CNET says it isn't a full coach that guides a training plan. MacRumors commenters called the demo "stiff". — **Medium**. — *Fix: coach messages must contain a specific, actionable delta (e.g. "add 2.5 kg to squat; you hit 3×8 at RPE 7 twice") rather than praise alone.* — [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/i-hate-apples-workout-buddy-already/); [BGR](https://www.bgr.com/2048525/watchos-26-workout-buddy-ai-features-disappointing/); [MacRumors forum](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/apple-unveils-ai-powered-workout-buddy-for-apple-watch-at-wwdc.2458440/) (2025)
- **Oura Advisor**: I found no independent user complaints. Hands-on reviews praise it as "not an intrusive or obnoxious tool" (Android Authority), say it "isn't pushed in the user's face" (Android Headlines), and like that it "checks in… every so often" (Android Central). Oura lets users choose the tone: "conversational" (supportive) or "direct" (goal-oriented). Oura's help notes answers are less personal on general topics such as women's or heart health. — *Implication: a tone setting and low intrusiveness are what reviewers reward; nütn should offer a tone toggle (gentle / direct / data-only).* — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/oura-advisor-hands-on-3472107/); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/wellness/fitness/ouras-ai-powered-advisor-is-rolling-out-heres-what-it-means-for-you); [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/wearables/oura-ring/oura-ring-5-review); [Oura blog](https://ouraring.com/blog/how-to-use-oura-advisor/) (2024–2025)
- **Gentler Streak** (positive tone signal): Digital Trends says rest days don't break your streak and the app "stops nagging you" when your body isn't ready. You can flag sickness, injury or vacation, which pauses the Activity Path. An App Store user says they "no longer feel lazy skipping a workout" when the app suggested rest. It won a 2024 Apple Design Award (Social Impact). — *Implication: a "pause / sick / injured / travelling" switch that freezes streaks and targets is a valued, low-cost feature.* — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/?p=3165528); [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gentler-streak-fitness-tracker/id1576857102)
- **Gentler Streak (accuracy, a tone-adjacent complaint)**: Yoga and strength sessions are rated "extremely light" even when "I'm sweating bullets". It flagged overreaching after telling the user to exercise. Users say "no hard numeric recovery score" makes coaching less actionable. — **Medium** (repeated across App Store reviews). — *Fix: let users log session RPE or effort to override HR-based load for strength and yoga, which nütn's lifting logs already capture.* — [Health App Insider 2026](https://www.healthappinsider.com/en/reviews/gentler-streak-review); [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gentler-streak-workout-tracker/id1576857102?see-all=reviews)

**Over-cautious refusals**
- I found no reports of AI fitness coaches refusing too often or over-hedging ("consult a doctor"). See Gaps.

**Privacy of health data sent to LLMs**
- **ChatGPT Health** (announced Jan 2026; one source says US rollout 2026-07-23): Privacy advocates note data shared with it "will not be protected by HIPAA". EPIC counsel said moving medical records in "would remove the HIPAA protection… which is dangerous" and that OpenAI "can change its terms at any time". CDT flagged that OpenAI is exploring advertising. OpenAI says the data is encrypted, isolated and not used to train foundation models. — **High in press, low in user reviews**. — *Fix: an LLM coach that is off by default and opt-in, with a per-request "what will be sent" preview, minimal context (only the needed days or metrics), no training use, and a local-only rule-based coach as the default.* — [The Record](https://therecord.media/chatgpt-health-draws-concern-privacy-critics); [EPIC](https://epic.org/the-record-chatgpt-health-feature-draws-concern-from-privacy-critics-over-sensitive-medical-data/); [Malwarebytes, Jan 2026](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2026/01/are-we-ready-for-chatgpt-health)
- **Research**: In the GPTCoach study "many participants expressed privacy concerns when integrating health data with a chatbot". A 2026 arXiv paper assesses health privacy risks in ChatGPT logs and memory. — [arXiv 2405.06061 (GPTCoach)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.06061); [arXiv 2609.14697](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14697)

**Cost of AI coaches**
- **Bevel Pro**: Hard to recommend at **$14.99/mo** if the AI coach is the only reason, and the weekly Intelligence allowance means it isn't unlimited. — **Low-Medium**. — *Fix: nütn's free rule-based coach plus an optional LLM; be explicit about any usage cap.* — [Autonomous.ai](https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/bevel-app-review)
- **Google Health Premium (Gemini coach)**: $9.99/mo or $99.99/yr standalone (third-party explainer, Sept 2026). Users who disliked it said they "cannot wait for my trial to end". — [Sensai explainer](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/google-health-premium-fitbit-ai-coach-2026) (vendor); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/fitbits-gemini-ai-coach-is-giving-users-unhinged-fitness-advice-heres-why-users-are-saying-they-cannot-wait-for-my-trial-to-end)

### Inferences
- The highest-trust differentiator for an LLM coach is **verifiable grounding**: every metric cited with a tap-through to the record, and an explicit "I don't have data for that" path. The Whoop threads show users catch fabrications by asking "where did that number come from?", so building that check in is cheap insurance.
- **Memory has become a visible, user-editable product surface** (Whoop My Memory, May 2026). nütn can match this with a "What the coach knows about you" screen of injuries, equipment, schedule, fixed activities (dog walks) and temporary states (sick or travelling until a date), each with a toggle. Because it is built from local logs, the privacy story is stronger.
- **Tone failures are symmetric.** Pushy optimisation (Fitbit) and empty cheerleading (Strava, Workout Buddy) both draw ridicule. The safe pattern is a user-chosen tone, a specific actionable delta, and default respect for rest and life constraints (Gentler Streak).
- **Rule-based classification with LLM explanation** avoids the Strava "150 bpm = anaerobic" class of error.

### Gaps
- No primary Reddit, App Store or Trustpilot pages could be read (blocked). All Reddit evidence is via journalists.
- **Over-cautious refusals or "consult a doctor" hedging**: no evidence found for any listed app.
- **Oura Advisor and Fitbod's AI**: no independent negative user reports found in 2024–2026 search results.
- **Claude used as a coach**: no complaint sources found specific to Claude (only ChatGPT).
- No quantitative prevalence data, such as the share of users reporting hallucinations; prevalence is judged from coverage only.
- Whether Fitbit's "modify plan on injury" works in practice is untested in any source I found.

## 2. What do users of human-coach apps (Future, Caliber, Ladder) value that apps lack?

### Takeaway
Users pay $149–$200/mo for Future and Caliber mainly for **a person who notices**: frequent check-in messages, someone "watching your data" who sees skipped workouts, and plans changed when life happens. Recurring downsides are **price**, **slow asynchronous form feedback** (video upload then wait), **coach turnover**, **long onboarding** and **coaching quality that varies by coach** (Ladder: "2 out of 10 or a 7 out of 10").

### Cited Findings
- **Future**: "My coach messages me almost every day, checking in to see how things went or what needs to be adjusted." — Valued feature: **daily check-ins and adjustment**. — **High** (the core pitch since the 2019 launch: "real coaches nag you"). — *Fix: a daily or after-session check-in prompt (2 taps: how it felt, anything hurt?) feeding the rule-based coach, plus a weekly review that proposes plan changes for the user to accept or reject.* — [Garage Gym Reviews, 2026](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/best-personal-training-apps) (secondhand; quote attributed to "a user in a recent review"); [TechCrunch 2019](https://techcrunch.com/2019/05/23/future-personal-trainer/embed/)
- **Future**: "Knowing a real person is watching your data and will notice if you skip workouts creates accountability that many users find genuinely transformative." — **High (as a valued trait)**. — *Fix: missed-session detection with a non-guilt nudge ("You planned Push on Tue; want to move it to today or skip this week?").* — [Sensai Future review 2026](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/future-app-review-2026) / [Cora Health compare](https://www.corahealth.app/compare/future) (secondhand; exact attribution between these two is uncertain)
- **Future (downsides)**: Coaches "cannot correct form in real-time; requires video and waiting". It may feel "impersonal" for those who want in-person energy. One reviewer had worked with "three different coaches", which suggests turnover. Price is $199/mo, or $149/mo prepaid yearly (some offers: $50 first month). — **Medium**. — *Fix: free in-app form cues and checklists per exercise; continuity, since the "coach" (rules plus logs) never changes or forgets.* — [Sensai](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/future-app-review-2026); [Cora Health](https://www.corahealth.app/compare/future); [Barbend](https://barbend.com/future-app-review/)
- **Caliber**: The coach was "incredibly responsive via the in-app chat, often replying within minutes", and coach switching is easy via support (Forbes). Drawbacks: "long onboarding process" and "clunky video uploads". An App Store reviewer cancelled a trial because the app was hard to use and the "contact us button didn't work". Premium is about $200/mo (Forbes: $600–$1,400 per 3 months depending on video calls). Pro group coaching is $19/mo. — **Medium**. — *Fix: a short onboarding, and a working in-app feedback or support path.* — [Sports Nerd 2025](https://sports-nerd.com/brand/caliber/); [Forbes Health](https://www.forbes.com/health/weight-loss/caliber-app-review/); [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/caliber-strength-training/id1482405410?see-all=reviews&platform=watch)
- **Ladder** (coach-led programmes, $29.99–$44.99/mo): A forum user said "you can get a 2 out of 10 or a 7 out of 10 on coaching". A trainer says new workouts each week make "true progressive overload… harder to achieve". There is "no real export of progress data", and saved workouts are limited on Pro. For people who train twice a week or ignore the prescribed loads, "your cost per effective workout gets silly". — **Medium**. — *Fix: stable exercise selection across a block with visible progression; full CSV export.* — [ClarkConnect forum](https://clarkconnect.com/forum/t/can-you-help-me-with-an-honest-ladder-fitness-app-review/2619); [Simply Strong trainer review](https://www.simplystrongapp.com/blog/ladder-app-review); [Outdoorsy Nomad 2026](https://www.outdoorsynomad.com/ladder-fitness-app-review/)
- **AI copying human check-ins**: Whoop launched "Proactive Check-Ins" (e.g. prioritise sleep before a key event, adjust training around travel) on 2026-05-08. — [WHOOP press](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20260508464188/en/WHOOP-Expands-Health-Platform-with-On-Demand-Clinician-Access-and-New-AI-Features)

### Inferences
- What people pay human coaches for maps onto features nütn can automate for free: (1) **check-in cadence** (daily micro, weekly review); (2) **noticing absence**, meaning missed planned sessions or logging gaps; (3) **plan change on life events**, such as travel, illness, a bad night or limited equipment today; (4) **continuity**, a coach that never changes and remembers everything. Human strengths nütn can't copy are real-time form correction and emotional relationship. The honest framing is to cover about 80% of the accountability loop and say so.
- The "travelling this week / only dumbbells today" adjustment is the specific moment users cite for human coaches. An equipment-aware swap ("I only have a hotel gym") is a high-value rule-based feature.

### Gaps
- No direct Future or Caliber Reddit threads were readable. No quantified churn reasons for human-coach apps were found.
- No data on what check-in frequency users prefer (daily vs. weekly), or on when check-ins become nagging. The only signal is Oura reviewers praising check-ins "every so often" and saying it isn't "pushed in your face".

## 3. What onboarding and paywall complaints come up (long quizzes, dark patterns, price hikes, trials)?

### Takeaway
The dominant onboarding pattern is a **10–20 minute personalisation quiz (25–113 screens) that ends in a locked "your plan" paywall with countdown timers** (Noom is the archetype; Cal AI and other apps copy it). Trust complaints cluster on **renewal and cancellation** (Centr, WW) and **broken promises** (Whoop's 2025 upgrade-fee U-turn). WW's Dec 2025 redesign produced quantified complaints: 30.3% of 1–3 star reviews were about billing.

### Cited Findings
- **Noom (onboarding length)**: Teardowns count up to **113 screens / 10–15 min** (RevenueCat; a careful pass took ~1.5 h), a **77-step** quiz (Screensdesign) and **96+ screens / 15–20 min** (a funnel-review dashboard). The quiz escalates to age, weight, eating habits and "emotional triggers", then shows a "personalized plan" reveal that requires payment. — **High (structural)**. — *Fix (nütn): onboarding under 2 minutes, with value before any ask (log first set or meal on screen 1); since nütn is free, say "No paywall, no trial, no card" up front.* — [RevenueCat teardown](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel.md); [Screensdesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/noom-weight-loss-food-tracker); [Twofold funnel dashboard](https://funnel-researcher-dashboard.internal.trytwofold.com/screenshots/noom) (an internal-looking domain; treat with caution)
- **Noom (dark patterns)**: A **15-minute countdown timer** to "reserve" the plan, prices broken into a weekly cost, and a "risk-free trial" that requires cancellation. One source calls the timer "manipulative", the kind of thing clinically aware audiences may see as a dark pattern. Trial length has varied (7, 14 days or shorter). — **High**. — [Rocketship HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/?p=5491); [Paddle "Fix That Funnel"](https://www.paddle.com/studios/shows/fix-that-funnel/noom); [Nutrola 2026](https://nutrola.app/en/blog/is-noom-free-anymore) (vendor/growth sources)
- **Industry norm**: A UK fitness app had a 45–50 screen / 7–8 min onboarding (RevenueCat). Cal AI shows "25+ onboarding cards" before the paywall (Adapty). Phiture research cited by Rocketship says 3–5 screens before the paywall beat both shorter and longer flows. A workout-app case study moved the paywall from session 1 to session 3. — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/why-your-onboarding-experience-might-be-too-short); [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/how-to-personalize-onboarding-and-paywalls-in-your-mobile-app/); [Rocketship HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/paywall-optimization-fitness-apps/); [Vmobify case study](https://vmobify.com/blog/fitness-app-marketing-case-study)
- **Caliber**: "long onboarding process". — **Low-Medium**. — [Sports Nerd](https://sports-nerd.com/brand/caliber/)
- **WeightWatchers (redesign Dec 2025; Canada Jan 2026)**: An analysis of **178** recent 1–3 star reviews found **30.3% about billing**, **20.2%** saying the rebuilt app takes "more taps than the old one", and **13.5%** reporting activity sync that "fails silently while reporting the device as connected". **16 reviews** describe a shift toward GLP-1 medication they "did not sign up for", and one says "you can't turn notifications off" about those programmes. ## 4. What do "all-in-one" users say about app sprawl vs. one app, and which integrations do they insist on?

### Takeaway
The evidence is thin and mostly vendor-adjacent. The consistent message is that serious users run a **nutrition app, a recovery tracker and a workout logger "that don't talk to each other"**, at roughly **$370–430/yr** combined, with **Apple Health as the fragile hub** (one-way writes, sync delays, silent failures). The integrations most often named are **Apple Health, Garmin, Oura, WHOOP and Strava**, plus Dexcom for CGM users.

### Cited Findings
- **Sprawl**: "Most serious fitness people run a nutrition app, a recovery tracker, and a workout logger, and… they don't talk to each other." MyFitnessPal Premium plus WHOOP plus Strong Premium costs approximately **$370–430 per year**. All-in-one apps used to lag because "nutrition tracking wasn't as deep as MFP", a gap now narrowing. — **Medium** (vendor blog). — *Fix: nütn's pitch is "the three apps in one, free", with nutrition depth at MFP level as the bar.* — [AskVora 2026](https://askvora.com/blog/best-apps-nutrition-recovery-workouts-2026) (vendor)
- **Strong ↔ MyFitnessPal**: Strong dropped its direct MFP integration and now routes through Apple Health. Strong's Health integration mainly writes workouts, and its read integration is limited. — *Implication: two-way Apple Health is required; read energy burned and write meals and workouts.* — [Strong Help Center](https://help.strongapp.io/article/146-myfitnesspal)
- **MyFitnessPal ↔ Apple Health**: An Apple Community thread reports "significant delays whenever anything tries to sync with Health". A commenter left MFP after complaining "over and over". — **Medium**. — *Fix: show sync status and the last-sync time per data type.* — [Apple Discussions 253927533](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/253927533)
- **WW**: 13.5% of negative reviews report sync that "fails silently while reporting the device as connected" (see Q3). — [Unstar](https://unstar.app/blog/weightwatchers-app-what-it-really-costs-reviews-2026)
- **Gentler Streak**: "Go Gentler doesn't measure my activity the same way my watch does and it's frustrating." Steps don't count as activity unless logged as workouts. — **Low-Medium**. — *Fix: reconcile with Apple Health totals, or explain any difference on screen.* — [Healthappinsider](https://www.healthappinsider.com/en/reviews/gentler-streak-review); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gentler-streak-fitness-tracker/id1576857102)
- **Bevel**: "Only as accurate as the Apple Watch data it reads", and it needs about two weeks to learn baselines. — [Autonomous.ai](https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/bevel-app-review)
- **Integrations commonly marketed by all-in-one competitors**: Apple Health, Oura, WHOOP, Garmin and Dexcom (Humuli), and "deep read/write" Apple Health (Cora). — [AskVora](https://askvora.com/blog/best-apps-nutrition-recovery-workouts-2026); [Cora Health](https://www.corahealth.app/blog/best-apple-health-fitness-apps) (vendors)
- **Older academic framing**: "Information and features are fragmented over too many apps, thereby limiting their usefulness." — [BMC Med Inform 2013 (PMC3621678)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3621678)
- **Ladder**: No real export of progress data. **Sweat**: guides are lost on cancel. Both show lock-in anxiety that feeds sprawl. — [ClarkConnect](https://clarkconnect.com/forum/t/can-you-help-me-with-an-honest-ladder-fitness-app-review/2619); [YEG Thrive](https://yegthrive.ca/sweat-kayla-app-review/)

### Inferences
- For nütn, the must-haves are **two-way Apple Health** (workouts, energy, weight, sleep, HRV and RHR in; workouts and nutrition out), **visible sync health**, and **import from Strong/Hevy/MFP CSV** so switching costs nothing. Garmin, Oura and WHOOP data mostly reach iOS through Apple Health, which is a pragmatic route for a free app.
- The emotional benefit of one app is **cross-domain explanations** (e.g. "your bench stalled the week your sleep averaged 5.9 h and protein was 30% under target"). Single-domain apps structurally can't do this, and nütn's grounded coach can.

### Gaps
- No Reddit or first-person user threads on sprawl were readable or surfaced. Most sources are vendors of all-in-one apps, so they are biased.
- No survey data on how many apps users run or which integration they'd refuse to give up.

## 5. What do workout-video and class apps (Fitness+, Centr, Sweat, NTC) get criticised for?

### Takeaway
Video and class apps are criticised for **no measured progression and no load logging** (Fitness+), **content that feels simple or repetitive** (Centr), **removed programmes and features after updates** (NTC training plans and offline mode), **losing access to bought programmes** (Sweat), and **reliability at the gym** (Centr not loading). The deepest structural complaint is that they deliver sessions, not a progression system.

### Cited Findings
- **Apple Fitness+**: The service "never measures or calculates any progression toward fitness goals". It can't pull equipment data (unlike Peloton). Content is best for beginners and intermediates, with labels limited to "absolute beginner" or typical. — **Medium**, but **dated (Dec 2020)**; I found no 2024–2026 user sources. — *Fix: log load and reps for strength work, including when following a video, and show progression per movement.* — [Fortune, 2020-12-14](https://fortune.com/2020/12/14/apple-fitness-plus-workout-app-review-cost)
- **Centr**: Workouts are "challenging, but simple and mundane", and the meditation and diet parts fall short (3-star App Store review). A Trustpilot reviewer described "arriving at the gym ready to go only to find your app isn't loading and you can't continue your workouts". — **Medium**. — *Fix: offline-first workout sessions (nütn is local-first).* — [Garage Gym Reviews / App Store snippet](https://www.garagegymreviews.com/centr-review); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/centr.com)
- **Nike Training Club**: A user "completed the Lean Fit Plan 3 times before the most recent update that removed the Training Plans". Another "would rather pay the premium price to bring back the plans that were removed". Version 6.72.0 (review dated May 2026) removed offline use and workout previews. Saved workouts disappeared after an update. One user's workout recorded "zero calories and… zero minutes". Users can no longer filter by workout type without scrolling. — **Medium-High** (repeated across review aggregators; some date back to 2021–22). — *Fix: users own their programmes; never remove a programme a user is enrolled in; templates persist locally.* — [JustUseApp NTC](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/301521403/nike-training-club/reviews); [AppSupports NTC](https://appsupports.co/301521403/nike-training-club/negative-reviews); [probleme.app](https://probleme.app/en/nike-training-club-problems/)
- **Sweat**: High price, "full of bugs and sometimes… extremely slow", and you lose guides when you cancel. — **Medium**. — [Fitnessdrum 2026](https://fitnessdrum.com/sweat-app-review/); [YEG Thrive](https://yegthrive.ca/sweat-kayla-app-review/)
- **Ladder** (coach-led class format): New workouts every week make "true progressive overload… harder". — [Simply Strong](https://www.simplystrongapp.com/blog/ladder-app-review)
- **Apple Workout Buddy** (the AI layer added to Watch workouts in 2025): Called a "pep talk" rather than coaching. — [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/i-hate-apples-workout-buddy-already/)

### Inferences
- nütn's lifting logger plus rule-based progression is the direct answer to the core video-app complaint. A possible bridge feature is letting users **log a class or video workout** (Fitness+, NTC) as a session with RPE and duration so it counts toward load and readiness, not only lifting.
- **Content and programme permanence** (NTC, Sweat) is a trust feature: a user's programmes and history stay with them.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 user complaints specific to Apple Fitness+ were surfaced (Reddit blocked; search returned only the 2020 Fortune review).
- No quantified data on how often "repetitive content" drives cancellation for Centr, Sweat or Fitness+.
