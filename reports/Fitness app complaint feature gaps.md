# Users Quit Over Broken Trust, Not Missing Features

Across workout trackers, food loggers and recovery/mood apps, the complaints that drive people away are mostly about broken trust, not missing features. The four that recur most are **lost data** (JEFIT, Gravl and Boostcamp sessions wiped mid-workout or after updates), **basics moved behind a paywall** (MyFitnessPal's barcode scanner, Strong's and Hevy's routine caps, Oura's, Whoop's and Garmin's subscriptions), **algorithms nobody can explain** (Fitbod's erratic load targets, RP's "random" volume changes, made-up recovery scores), and **friction at the moment of logging** (MFP's April 2026 redesign, ads mid-entry, no gram units). nütn already beats most of the field on the costly-to-build parts: it has no paywall and no ads, real periodised programming, a MacroFactor-style adaptive TDEE, offline-first storage, free export, and a mood engine with correlation analytics. Its gaps are mostly cheap "last-mile" items that users treat as table stakes. In the shipping build, **camera barcode scanning is not linked**, the **rest timer cannot alert on a locked phone**, there is **no gram/cup unit picker**, **drop sets cannot be entered**, the app **never writes to Apple Health**, and **account deletion leaves the auth user behind**. Several of these already have code paths in the repo. Fix the P0 items below and nütn's best pitch is credible: one free, trustworthy app that replaces Strong/Hevy, MacroFactor/Cronometer and Daylio. Every complaint below reached us secondhand through search excerpts, so read the priorities as directional, not measured.

## The evidence is secondhand, and much of it comes from rivals

The research environment blocked direct reads of **reddit.com, apps.apple.com, Trustpilot, the MyFitnessPal community and Cronometer forums** (HTTP 403 and DNS failures on 2026-10-08). Every user complaint here therefore comes from search-engine excerpts of those pages, not from full threads. **No verbatim Reddit thread was retrieved.** Reddit sentiment appears only where a third party reproduced it, and Cora Health's "Reddit quotes" are self-described paraphrases ([Cora Health](https://www.corahealth.app/blog/best-workout-tracker-reddit)). App Store and Play complaints arrive through aggregators (justuseapp, AppGrooves, mwm.ai, grand-screen). Each one is a single anecdote unless several source types repeat it.

A large share of the comparison content online is **written by competing app makers**. In workouts that includes Dr. Muscle, Sensai, Cora, PR Path, RepReturn, Push/Pull, GymGod, Setgraph, Volm, Mesostrength, StrengthLab360 and Arvo. In food it includes Nutrola, NutriScan, Fitia, Hoot, Bento Bunny, Amy Food Journal, gainframe, caleye, unstar.app and ai-food-tracker. MacroFactor's tap-count "speed index" is self-published and dates from 2022. This report marks those sources **(competitor blog)** in the text and **†** in the tables, and uses them only to corroborate. "Loudness" is a judgment based on how many independent kinds of source repeat a complaint; none of it is a measured frequency. The incumbents still hold high ratings: Strong about 4.9 on iOS from about 109k ratings, Hevy about 4.9 from about 192k ([aitoolsbakery Strong](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/strong-app-review/); [Hevy pricing](https://hevy.com/pricing)). The complaints come from a vocal minority, but they cluster tightly, and those clusters set the priorities below.

## Lifters leave over lost sets, silent timers and paywalled basics

**Data loss is the most damaging workout complaint, and every app in scope has at least one credible report of it.** A JEFIT user lost about **70 logged sessions** when cloud sync quietly stopped and a login problem blocked recovery ([justuseapp JEFIT](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/449810000/jefit-workout-planner-gym-log/reviews)). Gravl users lost everything after clearing data to fix a freeze, and lost saved workouts after an update; Gravl's own changelog admits "workouts failing to save" ([mwm.ai Gravl](https://mwm.ai/apps/personal-trainer-gravl/6450921637)). Boostcamp reset mid-workout on screen lock, and edits made on its web version reverted when the app opened ([Boostcamp App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/boostcamp-workout-programs/id1529354455?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)). Hevy's help centre warns that deleted workouts "cannot be recovered by our team" ([Hevy Help](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/33996260919703-Hevy-Apple-Watch-Sync-Issues-Step-by-Step-Troubleshooting-Guide)). Offline use is the matching wish. RP Hypertrophy reviewers beg "PLEASE make this app work WITHOUT an internet connection" ([RP App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rp-hypertrophy/id1555614554?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)), and indie trackers now market "basement gym, zero signal, no problem" ([Rakoon](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/rakoon)).

**The second cluster is the rest timer and the lock screen.** One Hacker News user went looking for a Strong replacement because it "hasn't kept pace with newer iOS features like Live Activities" ([HN 48932784](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48932784)). Strong's release notes now claim Live Activity support, so the complaint is partly stale. Boostcamp users say lock-screen progress "rarely shows up anymore", and its timer sound leaves music volume ducked until the app is force-closed ([Boostcamp reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/boostcamp-workout-programs/id1529354455?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)). Hevy shipped fixes for background alarms on Android 14, and its own help article walks users through several permission screens to get its Live Activity working ([Hevy Help: Live Activity](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/35649846517399-How-to-Use-Hevy-s-Live-Activity-on-iOS-and-Android)). The lesson is that **a timer that alerts reliably on a locked phone is table stakes**, and getting it wrong is the main source of frustration in the middle of a set.

**The third cluster is basics locked behind a paywall.** Strong's free tier caps users at **3 custom routines**, and its plate and warm-up calculators are PRO-only ([RepReturn](https://repreturn.com/strong-app-review/)†; [aitoolsbakery Strong](https://aitoolsbakery.com/blog/strong-app-review/)). Hevy's free tier allows **4 routines, 7 custom exercises and 3 months of charts**, and its warm-up calculator is Pro-only ([Sensai Hevy review](https://www.sensai.fit/blog/hevy-review-2026)†). Gravl reveals its **3-workout limit only after 10–15 minutes of onboarding** ([Gravl App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gravl-ai-personal-trainer/id6450921637?see-all=reviews)), and JEFIT locks its demo videos ([Dr. Muscle](https://dr-muscle.com/jefit-review-alternative/)†). Discoverability problems sit underneath this cluster. Hevy's FAQ has to explain where supersets are hidden ([Hevy set types](https://www.hevyapp.com/features/workout-set-types/)). A Hevy user wants mid-workout changes to "save & update routine" ([Hevy Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.hevy)). Gravl treats barbell and dumbbell hip thrusts as unrelated, so 200 lb on one became a 50 lb suggestion on the other ([Gravl reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/gravl-ai-personal-trainer/id6450921637?see-all=reviews)). Fitbod logged a one-hour session as **299 minutes**, and JEFIT writes similarly inflated durations to Apple Health when a "finish" is forgotten ([Fitbod reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fitbod-gym-fitness-planner/id1041517543?see-all=reviews); [AppGrooves JEFIT](https://appgrooves.com/ios/449810000/jefit-workout-planner-gym-log/jefit-inc/negative)).

Switching follows these complaints. Users move from Strong to Hevy for a more generous free tier, social and program sharing, and faster shipping, helped by Hevy's native Strong CSV import. **Routines do not transfer** in that import, which is why the community-built strong2hevy tool exists ([Hevy Help](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/38001424401943-How-to-Import-Strong-App-CSV-Files-and-Export-Your-Data-in-Hevy); [strong2hevy](https://github.com/criccomini/strong2hevy)). Users stay on Strong for speed and its lack of clutter, and they leave AI apps when the automation fights manual control.

## Food loggers punish bad entries, broken copy flows and paywalled scanners

**The longest-running food complaint is that users can't tell which database entry to trust.** One teardown of MyFitnessPal searched for a chain sandwich and got **eleven results spanning about 200 kcal**. It notes that a wrong pick "is silent and there is no signal to warn you" ([the-app-reviewer](https://the-app-reviewer.com/teardowns/myfitnesspal/)). Forum thread titles such as "Why are MFP food entries so wildly inaccurate?" keep recurring ([MFP Community](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10804613/why-are-mfp-food-entries-so-wildly-inaccurate)). Curated databases get the opposite complaint. One MacroFactor reviewer's partner "went back to myfitnesspal due to the abysmal food database" ([MacroFactor App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/macrofactor-macro-tracker/id1553503471?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)), and Cronometer users can't find fast-food items ([Cronometer forum](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/2470/how-to-input-restaurant-or-fast-food-options)). Coverage is the main reason people return to MFP; accuracy is the main reason they leave it. Neither model gives users what they actually ask for: "show me which entry to trust, and let me fix it". Yazio even confirms that a barcode "cannot be changed once it has been assigned to a food" ([Yazio Help](https://help.yazio.com/hc/en-us/articles/4576053290001-How-can-I-edit-public-foods)).

**The loudest recent friction event was MFP's own redesign.** Version 26.16.0 (April 21, 2026) replaced the Diary with cards and removed single-item copy. The forum lists further losses: "no longer possible to copy an entire day", and meals collapsed so every check takes an extra step. One analytics firm reported the rating falling **from 3.24 to 1.54** (a single, unverified source) ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/articles/app-updates/myfitnesspal-v26-16-0-replaces-diary-with-new-ui-sparking-rating-drop-in-april-2026); [MFP Community](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10957519/new-interface-flawed)). MacroFactor's pitch is the mirror image: frequent foods "float to the top" of each meal slot. Its self-published 2022 index counts **24 actions to MFP's 36** across four logging workflows ([MacroFactor](https://macrofactor.com/macrofactor-vs-myfitnesspal-2025/)†). Units are a smaller but stubborn complaint. MFP recipes yield only "servings", so users log "223 servings" of a 1,057-gram lasagna ([MFP Community](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10926417/allow-gram-option-when-creating-a-recipe)). MacroFactor users can't pick cups or tablespoons for some foods ([MacroFactor App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/macrofactor-macro-tracker/id1553503471?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)).

**Moving basics behind a paywall makes users angrier than a high price does.** MFP moved barcode scanning to Premium in about 2022. Premium now costs **$79.99 a year or $19.99 a month** ([MFP Community](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10847704/price-increase)). One analysis of 202 negative MFP reviews found **59 citing the paywall and 27 naming the scanner** ([unstar.app](https://unstar.app/blog/is-myfitnesspal-premium-worth-it-paywall-app-reviews-2026)†, which could not be opened). Lose It! moved its scanner to paid and paywalled daily macros ([Trustpilot Lose It!](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/loseit.com?page=2)). MFP's CSV export is Premium-only ([MFP Support](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032273352-Data-Export-FAQs)). Noom paid **$62M** in a 2022 class action over hard-to-cancel trials ([Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/noom-to-pay-over-60m-to-cancel-automatic-renewal-suit)), and BBB complaints about charges after cancelling continued into 2026 ([BBB](https://www.bbb.org/us/ny/new-york/profile/health-and-wellness/noom-inc-0121-150555/complaints)). Ads timed at the moment of entry are their own complaint. MFP shows full-screen, unskippable ads "to save food" ([FeastGood](https://feastgood.com/myfitnesspal-sucks/)), and Lose It! users describe ads "that highjack the screen just as you are trying to enter meal data" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/loseit.com?page=2)).

**Photo AI is the new expectation, and the research says it is biased low.** A July 2026 NIH-presented study ran 102 weighed meals through MFP, Lose It!, Cal AI and Appediet. All four **undercounted by about 250–345 kcal per meal** and missed about 30 g of fat ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/07/260726015237.htm)). A 2024 peer-reviewed test found Lose It! and FatSecret identified foods correctly only **46%** of the time ([Nutrients 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/16/15/2573)). The AI people do trust returns **editable, itemized entries**, as MacroFactor's does ([MacroFactor Help](https://help.macrofactorapp.com/en/articles/258-ai-food-logging)). A single opaque number earns less trust: Cal AI's re-scans return "a different wrong number" after a user corrects the portion ([calorierankings](https://calorierankings.com/reviews/cal-ai/)).

## Lifters and dieters trust rules they can see over AI they can't

Workout and nutrition coaching fail in the same way: a target moves and nobody explains why. Fitbod users report a lift "maxed out… at 13 reps at 50 pounds" followed by a prescription of 15 reps at 40 ([Autonomous](https://www.autonomous.ai/ourblog/fitbod-app-review)). One Fitbod user also found the app could not progressively overload the custom workouts they had built ([Dr. Muscle Reddit roundup](https://dr-muscle.com/fitbod-review-reddit/)†). RP's interface "hides the logic behind its decisions", and its auto-volume "often drives volume higher than you need" ([Ditchnet forum](https://ditchnet.org/t/can-anyone-share-an-honest-rp-hypertrophy-app-review/2143), low-quality source). Gravl reviewers say "the AI mode is terrible, but the PPL… splits work very well" ([Grand-Screen](https://grand-screen.com/apps/gravl-personal-trainer/reviews/)). JuggernautAI is accused of the opposite fault, getting "caught in a cycle" of prescribing the same exercises ([Dr. Muscle](https://dr-muscle.com/juggernaut-workout-app-review/)†). The trackers have their own version of the gap: Hevy auto-progresses only inside its generated programs, and Strong does not auto-progress at all ([Volm](https://www.volm.app/blog/hevy-vs-strong)†). One user built an open-source "hevy-autopilot" to add auto-progression to Hevy ([GitHub](https://github.com/aterreno/hevy-autopilot/issues/12)). At **about $35 a month**, JuggernautAI and RP get punished hard for bugs and billing friction. RP carries a 2.8 Trustpilot rating ([Mesostrength](https://mesostrength.com/blog/rp-hypertrophy-alternatives)†; [JustUseApp JuggernautAI](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1515756471/juggernautai/reviews)).

Nutrition coaching follows the same pattern. Static formula targets draw criticism, especially MFP's default floor of **1,200 kcal for women** ([MFP Support](https://support.myfitnesspal.com/hc/en-us/articles/360032625391-How-does-MyFitnessPal-calculate-my-initial-goals); [aworkoutroutine](https://www.aworkoutroutine.com/eating-back-calories-burned/)). Adaptive engines that ignore adherence earn praise. Reviewers say other apps make it "easy to quit and then binge", while MacroFactor keeps giving valid targets as long as the user keeps logging ([MacroFactor App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/macrofactor-macro-tracker/id1553503471)). Carbon users praise its visible check-in history, but complain that it waits a full week and then "keeps trying to lower calories too low" ([FeastGood Carbon](https://feastgood.com/carbon-diet-coach-review/); [Carbon App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/1437820611?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)). MacroFactor has no free tier, which shuts out people who want to try it first ([Bento Bunny](https://www.bentobunny.app/reviews/macrofactor-review)†). The pattern users reward in both domains is the same. They want **an established template or trend model, a deterministic rule, a one-line reason for every change, and an easy override**. Free-form AI generation without those draws the complaints above.

## Recovery, mood and engagement apps fail on made-up scores, guilt and privacy

Recovery wearables earn three complaints: scores that contradict how people feel, composite numbers experts call arbitrary, and subscriptions for data from hardware users already paid for. One Whoop user had "a series of greens when I've been ill, and a series of reds when I've felt great" ([TrainerRoad forum](https://www.trainerroad.com/forum/t/anyone-using-a-whoop/4491?page=26)). HRV researcher Marco Altini calls such composites "made-up scores" and cites a near-zero correlation between Garmin stress data and self-reported stress ([the5krunner](https://the5krunner.com/2025/08/19/garmin-body-battery-slammed-indirectly-by-altini-made-up-scores/)). Garmin Connect+ ($6.99/mo) drew a Reddit post with over **6,000 upvotes** calling it "bait and switch" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/health-fitness/smartwatches/furious-garmin-users-revolt-over-new-subscription-service-we-need-to-take-a-firm-stand)). Whoop reversed its 5.0 upgrade fees within two days of launch ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/11/fitness-tracker-whoop-faces-unhappy-customers-over-upgrade-policy)). Oura shipped "Rest Mode" and gentler messaging to answer score anxiety and orthosomnia ([AOL/Yahoo](https://www.aol.com/articles/oura-ring-judging-160543237.html)).

Mood trackers are praised for one thing above all: **correlation**, such as "rainy weather is linked to sadness" ([GummySearch](https://gummysearch.com/tools/best-products/mood-tracker-app/)). Their complaints are paywalled backups (Daylio), overload from too many trackables (Bearable), and data stored only on the device (How We Feel) ([Marlvel Daylio](https://marlvel.ai/apps/daylio-journal-mood-tracker-1/reviews); [ChoosingTherapy](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/bearable-app-review/); [AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/how-we-feel/1562706384?country=ch)). Privacy is a heavier issue here than in fitness. Mozilla found most mental-health apps failing basic standards, and the FTC fined BetterHelp **$7.8M** and Cerebral about **$7.1M** for sharing health data with advertisers ([Mozilla](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/privacynotincluded/articles/are-mental-health-apps-better-or-worse-at-privacy-in-2023/); [WSGR](https://wsgr.com/en/insights/ftc-announces-proposed-settlement-agreements-with-two-digital-health-companies-for-disclosing-consumers-health-information-to-third-party-advertisers-among-other-violations.html)). No mainstream app covers workouts, nutrition, recovery and mood together with depth. Apps that try are either dashboards with no logging or marketing claims nobody has tested ([AlternativeTo QS](https://alternativeto.net/category/sport-and-health/quantified-self)).

Engagement design has its own research base. Health and fitness apps keep only **about 3–8% of users at day 30** ([Business of Apps](https://businessofapps.com/data/health-fitness-app-benchmarks)). Peer-reviewed work shows that **broken streaks reduce later engagement**, most of all when the app points out the break and the user blames themselves; the authors advise against "you broke your streak" messages ([CU Boulder on Silverman & Barasch, JCR 2023](https://www.colorado.edu/business/news/2023/04/20/research-streaks-marketing-tech-barasch)). Calorie numbers carry real risk. **73% of eating-disorder patients who used MFP** said it contributed to their disorder ([Levinson et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5700836/)). Users of Cronometer, Yazio, Apple Fitness and Fitbit keep asking to hide calories while still tracking nutrients ([Cronometer forum](https://forums.cronometer.com/discussion/comment/11651); [Yazio board](https://yazioen.featureupvote.com/suggestions/349684/tracker-without-calorie-amount)). Data double-counting is a correctness problem, not a cosmetic one. Garmin, Whoop and Apple Watch can write the same calories through Apple Health, and the inflated "calories remaining" becomes a wrong eating target ([MFP Community](https://community.myfitnesspal.com/en/discussion/10835692/garmin-connect-calorie-adjustment-confusing); [Whoop Community](https://www.community.whoop.com/t/issue-with-step-count-duplication-in-apple-health/8056)). Trial length matters too. Trials of 17–32 days convert at **45.7%**, against **26.8%** for trials of four days or less ([RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)).

## Where nütn already wins

nütn's advantages are the expensive-to-build, structural ones its competitors charge for or lack entirely. **Price is the clearest.** Strong caps free routines at 3, Hevy caps routines, custom exercises and chart history, Gravl stops after 3 workouts, MFP charges $79.99 a year for a barcode scanner, MacroFactor has no free tier, and JuggernautAI and RP cost about $35 a month. nütn ships with **no paywall and no ad SDK**, and gives away warm-up sets, unlimited custom exercises and all-time analytics (`services/claudeCoach.js:L204`). **Programming depth** answers the most-cited limitation of the two most-loved loggers: Strong and Hevy "don't write programs". nütn already has about 21 proven templates, mesocycles, six progression models, deload detection and RP-style MEV/MAV/MRV volume tracking (`engine/progression.js`, `engine/workoutProgramEngine.js`). **Nutrition coaching** matches MacroFactor's adherence-neutral adaptive TDEE and adds diet-break detection and calorie cycling driven by training, for free (`engine/tdee.js`).

nütn also wins on **trust and data ownership**. It is offline-first, so workouts log in a basement gym (`services/offlineCache.js`). Accounts start anonymous, which answers the HN "no account" crowd. It offers a full JSON backup and CSV export where MFP charges for export, and it imports MFP and Cronometer history. Its legacy FEED already has the copy-meal, copy-day, template and frequent-food flows whose removal sank MFP's April 2026 rating. Its food search already sorts results into Gold, Verified and Community tiers. On the recovery and mood side, nütn is one of very few products that logs all four domains itself instead of only aggregating them. Its mood engine already computes correlations, and its weekly review refuses to report on thin data. Its REST score explicitly avoids fabricated defaults, a direct answer to the "made-up scores" critique (`engine/restScore.js`). Its clinical screeners, safety escalation and crisis resources go well beyond Daylio or Bearable. Social features are off by default, so the "forced feed" complaint does not apply.

| 7 | **Replace the "Don't break the chain" push** with total-days framing, and add a free numbers-off mode. | It is cheap, backed by research, and protects the ED-vulnerable minority. | R4, R3 |
| 8 | **Drop-set entry, per-set RIR and a free plate calculator.** | This fixes the "in the data model but no UI" failure and undercuts Strong's and Hevy's PRO features. | W3, W4 |

## Conclusion

The research changes the obvious strategy for an all-in-one app. Its job is not to out-feature Hevy, MacroFactor and Daylio one by one. Their users already rate them highly. What drives people away is a short list of trust failures: a lost session, a feature moved behind a paywall, a number that changed without a reason, an entry that turned out wrong, or a guilt-inducing push. nütn's architecture already avoids most of the structural versions of those failures: it is free, offline-first, exportable, has deterministic engines and keeps social features off. Its exposure is concentrated in the shipping build's last mile. A user who can't scan a barcode, misses a rest alert on a locked phone, or can't log 150 g of rice will never see the mesocycle engine or the mood correlations. The P0 list is therefore mostly about getting new users through their first week, and with day-30 retention at 3–8% across the category, that first week decides almost everything.

There are two strategic cautions. First, **MFP's April 2026 collapse is a warning about nütn's own v7 shell**. v7 currently drops copy-yesterday, meal templates and the custom-food builder. Making it the default before it reaches parity would repeat MFP's mistake, so keep it opt-in until those flows are native. Second, nütn's strongest unique claim, insight across workouts, food, sleep and mood, is also the one most open to the "made-up scores" critique. Correlations that show their sample size, confidence and a plain-language caveat will separate nütn from wellness pseudo-science. The same discipline applies to its readiness score. Because every complaint above reached us secondhand, the next research step is cheap and decisive: direct Reddit and App Store review mining once access is available, to turn these loudness judgments into counts.
